The University dedicated its new Center for Holistic Student Flourishing on Wednesday, in a ceremony attended by the trustees, the donors, the architects, and those students who were able to obtain tickets.

The Center, which cost forty million dollars and occupies the site of the former library annex, was described by the Provost as a decisive answer to the crisis in student well-being. It contains a three-story climbing wall, a meditation atrium, a room of unspecified purpose furnished with beanbags, and a juice bar operated by an outside vendor at market rates.

The University’s counseling service remains in the basement of the old infirmary, where it employs four counselors for nineteen thousand students. The wait for a first appointment is eleven weeks, a figure the administration noted has not increased.

> “We heard our students when they told us they were struggling. We responded with a building.”
>
> The Provost

Asked why none of the funds had been directed toward hiring counselors, the Vice President for Student Experience explained that counselors are a recurring expense, whereas a building is a legacy. She added that the atrium had been designed in consultation with a firm specializing in calm.

![Woodcut of a round-bodied creature striding along while blowing a horn.](/illustrations/pantagruel-09.webp)

The Center is the gift of an alumnus who made his fortune in student lending and whose name appears above the entrance, on each of the beanbags, and on the water. In his remarks he recalled his own years at the University as the happiest of his life, there having been at that time no fee for them to speak of.

To meet the cost of operating the building, the trustees have approved a Wellness Fee of four hundred dollars a term, payable by all students whether well or not. Students who find the fee distressing are encouraged to make use of the Center.

A sophomore interviewed outside the dedication said she had tried the climbing wall and found it very fine. She had gone in to ask whether anyone could see her about her nerves, and was given a harness.

The University announced that a second phase of construction will begin in the spring. It will add a nap pavilion and a wing for the administrators required to oversee the first phase.
