The City Council voted unanimously on Thursday evening to adopt a sweeping new transparency ordinance, according to a person familiar with the vote who asked not to be named because the meeting was not open to the public.

The measure, described by its sponsors as the most ambitious open-government reform in the city’s history, was debated for four hours behind the doors of the Council chamber, which had been closed for the occasion and fitted with a second set of doors.

Reporters waiting in the corridor were informed of the result by a clerk, who read a prepared statement confirming that a vote had occurred and that it had gone well.

> “The public has a right to know what we are doing. We intend to tell them at the earliest appropriate time.”
>
> The President of the Council

The President of the Council told this newspaper that the ordinance would transform the relationship between the government and the governed. Asked what the ordinance required, he said that this was precisely the sort of question it had been written to address.

Copies of the text may be requested in writing from the Office of Public Records. The Office confirmed that requests are answered in the order received and that it is presently answering those received in the spring of 2019.

![Woodcut of a figure whose head is an open pot, holding up a small whisk.](/illustrations/pantagruel-06.webp)

A summary of the ordinance was issued on Friday morning. It runs to eleven pages, nine of which have been withheld under an exemption the ordinance is believed to have created. The two remaining pages consist of a title and a list of the members present, three of whose names have been removed.

Members were nonetheless united in their praise. One called the measure “a new dawn.” Another called it “overdue.” A third said he had not read it but had been assured by colleagues that he would have liked it.

Civic groups have greeted the reform with caution. The League for Good Government said it would reserve judgment until it had seen the ordinance, and has since been advised that its judgment may be reserved indefinitely.

The Council will take up the matter of enforcement at its next meeting. The date of that meeting has not been announced, in keeping with the new rules.
