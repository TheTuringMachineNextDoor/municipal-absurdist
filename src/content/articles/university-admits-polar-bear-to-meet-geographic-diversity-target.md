The University announced on Tuesday that it has offered a place in its incoming class to a polar bear, bringing to fifty the number of regions represented on campus and completing a target the trustees set in 2019.

The bear, a male of about nine hundred pounds, was admitted from the Arctic, a region the Dean of Admissions described as historically underrepresented and difficult to recruit from by post.

> “We look at the whole applicant. In this case there was a great deal of applicant to look at.”
>
> The Dean of Admissions

The Dean said the bear had no test scores, no transcript, and no recorded activities outside of hunting, but that the committee had been moved by his story, which it pieced together from aerial photographs. His personal essay consisted of a seal.

The bear will be housed in the freshman dormitory. Residents have been sent a pamphlet titled *Living With Difference*, which asks them to keep an open mind and their doors shut.

Not everyone is pleased. A group of alumni wrote to object that the bear had taken a place from a qualified human applicant. The University replied that it could not comment on individual cases, and that the applicant in question is no longer available for comment either.

The bear has declared no major. Advisers say he has shown interest in marine biology, and in the advisers.

Tuition will be charged in full. The bear did not qualify for aid, the office explained, his parents being impossible to locate and therefore presumed wealthy.
