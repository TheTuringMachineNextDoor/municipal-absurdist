Warren Buffett told a gathering of shareholders on Saturday that the whole of his fortune could be traced to two habits formed early in life, namely the reinvestment of dividends and the refusal to be hurried through a soft drink.

Mr. Buffett was photographed during the meeting taking a measured sip from a bottle of cola that aides confirmed he opened in the spring of 1987. He has carried it to every annual meeting since.

> “People ask me when I plan to finish it. I tell them the same thing I tell them about stocks. My favorite holding period is forever.”
>
> Mr. Buffett, to shareholders

The bottle is understood to have gone flat during the first Bush administration. Mr. Buffett described this as a short-term fluctuation and said he had not looked at it closely in years, which he recommended as a general policy.

Analysts were impressed. One noted that at the present rate of consumption the soda would outlast most pension funds, and several of the analysts.

Asked whether younger investors might profit from his example, Mr. Buffett said the principle was simple and available to anyone. One has only to buy a good thing at a fair price, he explained, and then do nothing whatever for sixty years, a discipline he acknowledged most people abandon by the second afternoon.

Shareholders left the meeting encouraged. Many were seen buying a soda on the way out, and by evening most of them had finished it.
