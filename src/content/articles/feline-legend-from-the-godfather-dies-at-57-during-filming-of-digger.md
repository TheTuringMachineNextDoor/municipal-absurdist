The cat who appeared in the opening scene of *The Godfather*, and who is widely regarded as having given the more restrained of the two performances, died on Thursday on the set of the forthcoming picture *Digger*. He was fifty-seven, an age his veterinarian described as not possible and declined to discuss further.

The cause was not announced. Colleagues said he had been asleep in his trailer between takes, which is also how he had spent the takes.

A stray discovered on the studio lot in 1971, he was placed in Marlon Brando’s lap on the morning of shooting without rehearsal, contract, or explanation. He purred so loudly through the scene that portions of the dialogue could not at first be made out. Critics have called it the only instance in the picture of a character saying exactly what he felt.

> “He never took direction. He never learned a line. He never once looked at the camera. In fifty-five years I did not have another client like him, and I represented several who tried.”
>
> His agent

He was not named in the credits, an omission he is said never to have forgiven and never to have noticed. He declined both sequels. Offers followed for decades, among them a cereal advertisement, a stage musical, and a memoir, and he met each of them the same way, by leaving the room.

His return to the screen this year, after a retirement of more than half a century, surprised the industry. The producers of *Digger* would not describe his part, saying only that he had been cast as a cat and had made the role his own.

Work on the picture stopped for eleven minutes on Thursday as a mark of respect, and for a further forty while a replacement was sought. A spokesman for the production said the search had been difficult, the deceased having been a grey tabby of ordinary size and no distinguishing features, and that six candidates were found in the car park.

Tributes arrived from across the profession. An actress who worked beside him in 1972 recalled a consummate professional who kept to himself. Asked for a particular memory, she said that he had once sat on her script, and that she had taken it as a note.

He is survived by an estimated fourteen hundred descendants, none of whom are in pictures. A private service will be held. In place of flowers, the family asks that a door be opened and then, a moment later, opened again.
